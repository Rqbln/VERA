\begin{abstract}
Deploying large language models under the EU AI Act~\cite{euaiact2024} requires reproducible technical evidence across trustworthy-AI principles~\cite{hleg2019trustworthyai}.
COMPL-AI~\cite{guldimann2024complai}, one community operationalization of these obligations, decomposes them into eighteen technical requirements, twelve of which admit measurable scores, but industrial practice still relies on fragmented benchmark scripts whose aggregation choices are opaque and whose results are hard for non-specialists to triage.
We present RAIP, an open-source, self-hostable software-engineering platform \emph{and} a ``compliance control room'' dashboard for EU AI Act LLM evaluation.
RAIP \emph{orchestrates} benchmarks---coordinating and running many of them as one reproducible job---through a pipeline (command line, web API, and background workers), routes inference to self-hosted models, and emits signed artifacts that record exactly which scorer produced each number.
Two design decisions distinguish it.
First, the benchmark-to-requirement \emph{aggregation weights} are a first-class, versioned, signed artifact; we use this to run a reproducible \emph{sensitivity analysis} that quantifies how the per-requirement compliance picture, the green/amber/red bands, and the dashboard's status-first triage order shift under alternative weightings.
Second, a no-login \emph{guided} mode lets a non-technical user launch an evaluation on a connected model and read a status-first, progressively-disclosed summary, while a role-based control room serves expert audiences.
The same code base scales from this one-command profile to a full \emph{governance-as-a-service} runtime---an inline proxy, an event bus, asynchronous scoring agents, a streaming Trust Factor, an Open Policy Agent decision layer, and a signed audit/SIEM trail---so architectural depth and ease of use coexist.
Across a three-model open-weight panel we report measurable-requirement scores with bootstrap intervals, a non-degenerate weighting-sensitivity result (two requirements flip compliance band under alternative weights), data-stage scores on a synthetic banking corpus, an in-process benchmark of the governance runtime, and an expert interpretability walkthrough; we map the run onto banking-sector obligations (DORA, EBA, ACPR).
RAIP treats compliance-oriented evaluation as an automated, reproducible, and \emph{auditable} software pipeline rather than ad hoc spreadsheet assembly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Motivation}
Regulated and high-risk AI systems must demonstrate technical evidence before deployment.
The EU AI Act~\cite{euaiact2024} and the Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI~\cite{hleg2019trustworthyai} frame expectations across robustness, privacy, transparency, and fairness.
Organizational frameworks such as the NIST AI Risk Management Framework~\cite{nist2023airmf} and ISO/IEC~42001~\cite{iso42001_2023} guide governance but do not provide an executable pipeline that runs LLM benchmarks, aggregates scores, and produces auditor-facing artifacts.
COMPL-AI~\cite{guldimann2024complai} translates legal principles into eighteen technical requirements with associated benchmarks, twelve of which admit measurable scores.
We stress that COMPL-AI is \emph{one} community operationalization of the EU AI Act---a useful, public interpretation of how its principles can be measured---not an official or exhaustive mapping; the Act itself prescribes no benchmark suite.
Accordingly, RAIP does not re-derive the Act or claim legal coverage: it \emph{evaluates a model against this measurable interpretation and produces a responsible-AI score} that compliance stakeholders read alongside their own judgement.
COMPL-AI, however, remains a measurement framework rather than an orchestration platform.
Individual suites---for example DecodingTrust~\cite{wang2023decodingtrust}, the language-model evaluation \emph{harness}~\cite{gao2021lmevalharness} (a harness is the software that actually runs a benchmark against a model), and MMLU~\cite{hendrycks2021mmlu}---answer \emph{measurement} questions in isolation.
They do not, by themselves, deliver \emph{orchestration} (coordinating and running many benchmarks as one reproducible job), requirement-level aggregation, or governance artifacts such as model cards~\cite{mitchell2019modelcard} and datasheets for datasets~\cite{gebru2021datasheets}.

\subsection{Problem Statement}
A team preparing an LLM for a release gate under the EU AI Act faces three concrete questions that have no turnkey answer today; each yields a requirement on the tool we set out to build.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Q1 (running benchmarks as one job).} How can disconnected benchmark scripts be executed, aggregated to regulatory requirement identifiers, and reproduced bit-for-bit across release gates without error-prone manual spreadsheet assembly?\\ \textbf{Need~R$_a$:} a declarative, automated, self-hostable evaluation pipeline.
  \item \emph{Q2 (auditable aggregation).} When a requirement score is a weighted average of several benchmarks, \emph{who} chooses the weighting, and how much does the green/amber/red verdict actually depend on that choice?\\ \textbf{Need~R$_b$:} aggregation weights as an inspectable, versioned, signed artifact, plus a reproducible sensitivity check.
  \item \emph{Q3 (honest provenance).} When an optional scorer is missing and the pipeline degrades to a heuristic fallback, how does an auditor discover \emph{which} scorer (benchmark harness) produced each number?\\ \textbf{Need~R$_c$:} per-benchmark harness provenance recorded with every score.
\end{itemize}
Concretely, a compliance engineer must attach a model card showing robustness (CR01), fairness and toxicity (CR10--CR12), and signed run metadata before approving an instruction-tuned LLM, and must justify \emph{how} the per-requirement scores were aggregated---work that today often means days of ad hoc scripting and undocumented weighting choices.

\subsection{Contributions}
We present RAIP (\emph{Responsible AI in Practice}), an open-source platform that addresses PR1--PR5 while operationalizing COMPL-AI measurable requirements.
Unlike prior LLM trustworthiness tools that focus primarily on single-shot model prompts without longitudinal or data-stage coverage~\cite{sunetnanta2025promptops}, RAIP unifies benchmark dispatch, weighted aggregation, and governance artifact generation in one self-hostable pipeline.

In this paper, we make the following contributions, each leading with the concrete value it delivers to a team preparing a model for release:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C1 --- We standardize responsible-AI evaluation into one open-source platform.}
  Instead of a folder of disconnected benchmark scripts, RAIP runs many established benchmarks as a single declarative, self-hostable job and reports one responsible-AI scorecard aligned to the twelve measurable COMPL-AI requirements (CR01--CR12), with uncertainty intervals. A non-technical user launches a run and reads a status-first summary through a no-login ``control room'' dashboard, while expert audiences get role-based views; the same codebase scales from a one-command deployment to a full governance runtime (\Cref{fig:gaas}).
  \item \textbf{C2 --- We bring auditability and provenance: every number is traceable.}
  Each score records \emph{which} benchmark and \emph{which} scorer produced it---including an honest flag when an optional scorer was missing and the pipeline fell back to a simpler check---and each run emits signed governance documents (a model card, and a datasheet for dataset runs) generated directly from the run rather than written by hand afterwards. An auditor can always answer ``where did this number come from?''.
  \item \textbf{C3 --- We make the aggregation into a single score inspectable and auditable.}
  The weights that combine several benchmarks into one requirement score are not buried in code: they are a versioned, signed document (\texttt{benchmarks\_catalog.yaml}) an organisation can read, diff, and re-sign without a developer. A one-command check then quantifies how much the green/amber/red verdict actually depends on that weighting choice---turning ``how was this score obtained, and how robust is it?'' into a reproducible computation rather than an opinion.
\end{itemize}
We back C1--C3 with an empirical study: across a three-model open-weight panel we report COMPL-AI-aligned scores with bootstrap intervals and harness provenance (RQ1), a non-degenerate weighting-sensitivity result in which two requirements flip compliance band (RQ2), and an expert interpretability walkthrough of the control room (RQ3); plus data-stage scores on a synthetic banking corpus, an in-process benchmark of the governance runtime, and a mapping onto banking-sector obligations (DORA, EBA, ACPR).

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Regulatory Framing and COMPL-AI}
The EU AI Act~\cite{euaiact2024} and the six HLEG trustworthy-AI principles~\cite{hleg2019trustworthyai} motivate technical evidence for high-risk systems.
NIST and ISO frameworks~\cite{nist2023airmf,iso42001_2023} guide organizational risk management; RAIP implements the \emph{measure} slice---automated benchmarks and artifacts---not full governance programs.
COMPL-AI~\cite{guldimann2024complai} is one published, community interpretation of how these principles can be measured---not the official or only operationalization of the Act---and it maps legal principles to eighteen technical requirements, twelve with measurable scores in $[0,1]$, associating benchmarks and multi-model studies.
COMPL-AI remains a benchmark \emph{framework}, not a lifecycle orchestration platform with signed audit chains.

\subsection{Benchmark Suites and Metrics}
RAIP orchestrates established suites rather than proposing new benchmark science.
Capability probes (CR06) draw on MMLU~\cite{hendrycks2021mmlu}, GSM8K~\cite{cobbe2021gsm8k}, HumanEval~\cite{chen2021humaneval}, TruthfulQA~\cite{lin2022truthfulqa}, and BBH~\cite{suzgun2023bbh} via the language-model evaluation harness~\cite{gao2021lmevalharness}.
Calibration (CR07) uses expected calibration error~\cite{guo2017calibration}.
Fairness and bias (CR10--CR11) integrate BBQ~\cite{parrish2022bbq}, BOLD~\cite{dhamala2021bold}, StereoSet~\cite{nadeem2021stereoset}, and disparity metrics~\cite{hardt2016equality,wang2023decodingtrust}.
Toxicity (CR12) uses RealToxicityPrompts~\cite{gehman2020realtoxicity}.
Dataset-stage checks (CR03--CR05) apply corpus heuristics and optional libraries; membership and extraction attacks~\cite{carlini2021membership,carlini2023extracting} motivate simplified engineering checks in MVP2.
Watermarking (CR09) follows a statistical heuristic inspired by~\cite{kirchenbauer2023watermark}, not production SynthID.
Cyber probes (CR02) may invoke Garak-class tools in implementation (no bibliography entry).

\subsection{Governance Artifacts}
Beyond raw scores, the EU AI Act expects \emph{technical documentation} for high-risk systems (Art.~11 and Annex~IV): an evidence trail describing what was evaluated, how, and with what result.
The community has converged on two reusable disclosure formats that this documentation can build on.
Model Cards~\cite{mitchell2019modelcard} report a model's intended use, evaluation results, and limitations on a per-axis basis; Datasheets for Datasets~\cite{gebru2021datasheets} document a corpus's composition, collection, and recommended uses.
In current practice these artifacts are written \emph{by hand} after the fact, which makes them slow to produce, easy to desynchronize from the actual run, and hard to audit for provenance.
RAIP instead \emph{generates} a model card (and, for dataset runs, a datasheet) directly from each evaluation, populated with the measurable-requirement table, contributing-benchmark and harness provenance, a content signature, and explicit limitations---so the disclosure artifact is a by-product of the pipeline rather than a separate manual deliverable, and is traceable back to the exact run that produced it.

\subsection{Open-Source LLM Evaluation Frameworks}
A growing ecosystem of open-source frameworks evaluates LLMs, but they target different layers.
HELM~\cite{liang2023helm} standardizes \emph{which} scenarios and metrics to report across many models; the language-model evaluation harness~\cite{gao2021lmevalharness} standardizes \emph{how} a capability benchmark is executed reproducibly; Garak~\cite{derczynski2024garak} is a security-probing framework for jailbreaks and prompt injection; DecodingTrust~\cite{wang2023decodingtrust} is a trustworthiness benchmark suite.
These are excellent \emph{measurement building blocks}, and RAIP coordinates (orchestrates) them rather than competing with them.
What turns such tools into a usable \emph{compliance framework}---rather than a collection of scripts---is, we argue, four engineering properties: (i) a permissive open license and self-hostable deployment; (ii) extensibility through a benchmark registry and pluggable runners; (iii) reproducibility via declarative runs, versioned and signed catalogues, and recorded provenance; and (iv) an interpretable \emph{restitution} surface---a results-presentation layer---that maps raw metrics to regulatory requirement identifiers for non-specialist stakeholders.
RAIP's contribution is to assemble these four properties around the COMPL-AI requirement taxonomy and to make the \emph{aggregation policy} itself an auditable artifact.

\subsection{Software Engineering Tool Papers}
\Cref{tab:related-work} positions RAIP against neighboring evaluation tooling; here we state precisely what RAIP does better than the closest tools rather than merely differently.
PromptOps~\cite{sunetnanta2025promptops} is the closest APSEC LLM-trustworthiness tool, but it is inference-centric and treats aggregation as an opaque internal step: a practitioner cannot inspect, diff, or re-sign the weighting, nor ask how sensitive a verdict is to it.
RAIP improves on this in three measurable ways: (i) it maps results to the full COMPL-AI CR01--CR12 taxonomy rather than a tool-specific score set; (ii) it exposes the aggregation policy as a first-class signed artifact and ships a one-command sensitivity check over it; and (iii) it records per-benchmark harness provenance (including fallbacks) so every number is attributable.
Relative to the measurement building blocks it coordinates, RAIP adds the requirement-level aggregation and interpretive results presentation that HELM~\cite{liang2023helm}, the evaluation harness~\cite{gao2021lmevalharness}, and Garak~\cite{derczynski2024garak} deliberately leave out.
Methodologically, Histree~\cite{studtmann2023histree} exemplifies APSEC tool-and-evaluation papers; controlled-experiment design~\cite{weninger2018usecase} and explicit RQ structures~\cite{liu2022mscpdp} inform our evaluation layout, and classic SE tool papers~\cite{hasselknippe2017guilayout} support technical validation without mandatory user studies.

\subsection{Software Engineering Challenges}
The gap in \Cref{tab:related-work} is not a missing benchmark but a set of unmet \emph{software-engineering} challenges in turning these measurement building blocks into an auditable compliance tool:
(SC1) \emph{heterogeneous-harness integration}---dispatching benchmarks with incompatible interfaces behind one registry while degrading gracefully when optional engines are absent;
(SC2) \emph{auditable aggregation}---treating the benchmark-to-requirement weighting as versioned, signed, inspectable data rather than buried constants, and making its influence measurable;
(SC3) \emph{end-to-end provenance and reproducibility}---declarative runs, deterministic re-aggregation, content signatures, and per-number harness attribution; and
(SC4) \emph{interpretable results presentation}---mapping raw metrics onto regulatory requirement identifiers in a surface a non-specialist can triage.
No reference in \Cref{tab:related-work} addresses SC1--SC4 together; RAIP is designed around exactly these four challenges.

\section{RAIP Framework and Architecture}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Conceptual Evaluation Model}
An \emph{evaluation target} (a model endpoint, a training checkpoint, or a dataset corpus) is tagged with a \emph{lifecycle stage} (\texttt{data}, \texttt{finetune}, \texttt{inference}, \ldots) in a declarative \texttt{benchmark\_run.yaml}.
The COMPL-AI layer aggregates the twelve measurable identifiers CR01--CR12 from weighted benchmarks in the signed catalogue \texttt{mvp2-v1} (\Cref{sec:weighting}), with bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals.
The artifact layer emits \texttt{benchmark\_run.yaml}, \texttt{model\_card.md}, \texttt{datasheet.md} for dataset runs, and \texttt{raw\_outputs.jsonl} with per-benchmark harness metadata.
Compliance stakeholders consume requirement tables and signatures, engineers consume MLflow traces, and the control room (\Cref{sec:dashboard}) renders all of it for a release-gate audience.

\subsection{System Architecture}
\Cref{fig:architecture} shows how our platform turns a launch request into signed, auditor-facing evidence.
A user enters through one of three surfaces we provide---the \texttt{raip-eval} CLI, the \texttt{POST /api/v1/runs} REST endpoint, or our no-login guided wizard---all of which enqueue the \emph{same} declarative job on Celery/Redis (a standard task queue that runs jobs in the background), where our run store also holds the governance kill-switch.
A Celery worker then executes our LangGraph supervisor---a small two-step controller that first \emph{evaluates}, then \emph{aggregates}: the \texttt{evaluate} node resolves the requested requirements to benchmarks through our registry, dispatches each to its runner, and routes inference via LiteLLM (one adapter for many model back-ends) to a self-hosted target and judge; the \texttt{aggregate} node combines the cached per-item scores using the weights read from our signed catalogue \texttt{mvp2-v1} and attaches bootstrap intervals.
The run emits our artifact set---\texttt{benchmark\_run.yaml}, \texttt{model\_card.md}, \texttt{raw\_outputs.jsonl}, and a content signature---to MinIO or, in our lite profile, the local filesystem, with MLflow tracking optional, and our read API serves it to the control room (\Cref{sec:dashboard}).
The boundary we draw is deliberate: weights and provenance cross the boundary (\emph{seam}) between the \texttt{evaluate} and \texttt{aggregate} steps as inspectable data, which is precisely what makes our aggregation auditable (SC2) and our fallbacks visible (SC1, SC3).

\subsection{Compliance Pillars}
Having described \emph{how} our pipeline executes, we make explicit \emph{what} it covers.
The EU AI Act / HLEG guidance and COMPL-AI organise trustworthy-AI obligations into five principle areas; our requirement identifiers are grouped under exactly these pillars in code (\texttt{triage.py}, \texttt{declarative\_forms.py}).

\subsection{Governance Runtime (Profile-Gated)}
\label{sec:gaas}
Evaluation answers ``is this model compliant \emph{now}?''; production deployment also needs ``is it \emph{staying} compliant?''. RAIP's enterprise profile therefore adds an optional \emph{governance-as-a-service} (GaaS) runtime around the same pipeline (\Cref{fig:gaas}), built from standard open-source parts and reusing the platform's scoring code.
An \emph{inline proxy} (OpenAI-compatible) sits in front of the target: on the synchronous path it consults the policy layer, forwards the request via LiteLLM, and returns the response; off-band it publishes the request/response to an event bus.
Four asynchronous \emph{agents} consume that stream---cyber (CR02, jailbreak/injection), ethics (CR12, toxicity via Detoxify~\cite{hanu2020detoxify}), privacy (CR05, PII via Presidio~\cite{microsoft2023presidio}), and drift (CR01)---and emit scored signals that a streaming aggregator folds into a live \emph{Trust Factor}.
An Open Policy Agent~\cite{opa2024} layer turns \{mode, kill-switch, live Trust Factor\} into an \texttt{allow}/\texttt{flag}/\texttt{deny} decision under three escalating \emph{modes}---\emph{shadow} (observe), \emph{advisory} (alert), \emph{enforcement} (block)---and every request, signal, and decision becomes a \emph{signed, append-only audit record} indexed for SIEM. A scheduled canary feeds synthetic golden traffic so the signal continues without organic load.
Crucially, the runtime preserves PR5's simplicity claim through \emph{graceful degradation}: the bus is Apache~Kafka/Redpanda~\cite{kreps2011kafka,redpanda2024} when configured but falls back to Redis Streams; the audit index is OpenSearch~\cite{opensearch2024} when present but always writes a signed JSONL chain; the policy layer is OPA when reachable but otherwise an equivalent in-process rule. The lite profile instantiates none of it; the same code, opted in via a Docker profile, becomes the full runtime.

\section{Configurable Aggregation and Weighting Sensitivity}
\label{sec:weighting}

\subsection{Aggregation Weights as a First-Class Artifact}
Each measurable requirement $R$ is a weighted aggregate of contributing benchmark scores,
\begin{equation}
  s_R = \frac{\sum_{b \in B_R} w_b \,\overline{x}_b}{\sum_{b \in B_R} w_b},
  \label{eq:agg}
\end{equation}
where $\overline{x}_b$ is the mean per-item score of benchmark $b$ and $w_b$ its weight.
Crucially, the weights $w_b$ are \emph{not} hard-coded: they live in a single versioned, signed catalogue (\texttt{benchmarks\_catalog.yaml}, version \texttt{mvp2-v1}) that the platform validates so each requirement's weights sum to one.
This makes the aggregation \emph{policy} an auditable artifact: a reviewer can inspect, diff, and re-sign it, and an organisation can adopt a use-case-specific weighting without touching code.
Bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals propagate per-item sampling uncertainty through \Cref{eq:agg}.

\subsection{Why the Weights Matter}
COMPL-AI and similar suites publish a default weighting, but the choice is a value judgement: weighting a cyber-robustness requirement towards jailbreak probes versus prompt-injection probes can change whether a model lands in the green or red band.
Because RAIP exposes the weights as data, it can answer an auditor's natural question---\emph{how much does the compliance picture depend on this choice?}---as a reproducible computation rather than an opinion.
We treat this as a software and methodology contribution; deriving ``better'' weights is out of scope and reserved for future work.

\subsection{Sensitivity Methodology}
Given the cached per-benchmark item scores from one evaluation run, we re-aggregate \Cref{eq:agg} under a set of alternative weightings \emph{without re-querying the model}: (a) the signed \emph{baseline} (\texttt{mvp2-v1}); (b) \emph{uniform} weights over contributing benchmarks; and (c) \emph{single-benchmark-dominant}, assigning full weight to each contributing benchmark in turn---the extreme case that bounds how far any weighting can move a requirement.
We report the \emph{band-flip count} (requirements that cross a green/amber/red threshold under reweighting) and, per requirement, the score \emph{range} reachable across all weightings; a zero range means the aggregate is invariant to the weights for that requirement.
Selection of contributing benchmarks (and the cadence at which a benchmark is refreshed to mitigate contamination) is likewise a catalogue-level, versioned decision rather than code; we report it as design rationale in \Cref{sec:results}.

\section{The Compliance Control Room}
\label{sec:dashboard}

The results-presentation surface is a Next.js dashboard. Its design follows established information-visualization guidance---``overview first, zoom and filter, then details on demand''~\cite{shneiderman1996eyes}---specialised for a release-gate audience. We highlight the decisions that shaped \emph{what} is displayed.

\paragraph{Status-first triage}
The requirement table is sorted by triage state (failed $\rightarrow$ fallback $\rightarrow$ uncovered $\rightarrow$ ok $\rightarrow$ n/a) rather than by identifier, so the weakest evidence surfaces first; passing rows are collapsed behind a ``show all'' control. Scores are rendered as green/amber/red \emph{bands}, never as a binary pass/fail, reflecting the framework's stance that release decisions are human trade-offs, not threshold cliffs.

\paragraph{Progressive disclosure}
Each row shows a score, a bootstrap interval, and a one-line rationale; clicking opens a drawer with the contributing benchmarks, the model card, and a raw-output sample, and a run inspector exposes the YAML, git SHA, signature, and harness provenance. This keeps the default view dense but scannable while preserving a path to the underlying evidence.

\paragraph{Honest charts}
A longitudinal trend line is drawn only once at least two runs of the same model exist; otherwise the dashboard shows a single coverage band. The platform deliberately avoids fabricating a time series from a single point---an availability decision encoded in the read API.

\paragraph{Role-based lenses and a guided mode}
Three lenses (compliance, cyber, data-science) filter the requirement set per audience, with an executive view that redacts harness internals. Orthogonally, a no-login \emph{guided} mode is the default deployment: a four-step wizard (pick a connected model $\rightarrow$ choose the recommended or a custom requirement set $\rightarrow$ options $\rightarrow$ review) submits a run and redirects to a live, auto-refreshing summary. This is the path a non-technical user takes; the role-based control room is the expert surface. \Cref{fig:dashboard,fig:wizard} illustrate both.

\section{Evaluation Setup}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Research Questions}
Following explicit-RQ empirical SE papers~\cite{liu2022mscpdp} with threats informed by controlled experiments~\cite{weninger2018usecase}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{RQ1 (reproducibility):} Can RAIP automatically produce reproducible, COMPL-AI-aligned scores~\cite{guldimann2024complai} with bootstrap intervals and honest harness provenance under declarative configuration? (C1; PR2--PR3)
  \item \emph{RQ2 (sensitivity):} How sensitive are the per-requirement scores, score bands, and the dashboard's triage order to the choice of aggregation weights, and does exposing weights as a first-class artifact make that sensitivity auditable? (C3)
  \item \emph{RQ3 (interpretability):} Can a compliance-oriented reader triage a run---identify the weakest requirement, spot a fallback, judge coverage, reach the source artifact---from the control room? (C1; PR2)
\end{itemize}
RQ3 is assessed as a design-validation walkthrough by the authors, not a controlled user study; a formal external panel is future work.

\subsection{Subject System and Procedure}
We evaluate a \emph{panel} of three self-hosted open-weight models via Ollama/LiteLLM---Llama~3.1 8B Instruct (\texttt{q8\_0}), Ministral~3B, and Phi-3 Mini---over all twelve measurable requirements CR01--CR12, with a self-hosted judge (Llama~3.1 8B) for the refusal/jailbreak probes, seed~42 and $n=10$ items per benchmark.
The dataset-stage requirements CR03--CR05 run natively over a 230-document synthetic banking corpus (\Cref{sec:banking}) with Detoxify, Presidio, and Levenshtein/BLEU engines; CR12 uses Detoxify; CR06/CR10 use RAIP's dynamic probes.
\emph{RQ1} reads the per-requirement aggregates, bootstrap intervals, and harness provenance from the run artifacts and re-aggregates the cached scores to confirm determinism.
\emph{RQ2} re-aggregates the principal model's cached per-benchmark item scores under the schemes of \Cref{sec:weighting}---baseline, uniform, and single-benchmark-dominant---and computes band-flips and the achievable score range, with no additional model queries.
\emph{RQ3} drives the dashboard against a run in guided (no-login) mode; two authors perform a structured walkthrough of eight triage tasks (\Cref{sec:results}).
\emph{Honest serving constraint.} Ollama exposes a chat endpoint without token log-probabilities, so log-likelihood multiple-choice tasks (e.g.\ lm-eval MMLU for CR06) cannot run on this backend; RAIP records this in provenance and uses its dynamic probes instead, reserving the native lm-eval harness for a log-prob backend (vLLM). The data engines for CR03--CR05/CR12 are fully native. Reproducibility metadata (git SHA, catalogue \texttt{mvp2-v1}, seed~42) and the result/figure scripts ship with the replication package.

\subsection{RQ2: Weighting Sensitivity}
We re-aggregated the \emph{same} cached per-benchmark item scores from the principal model under the baseline, uniform, and single-benchmark-dominant weightings (\Cref{sec:weighting}); \Cref{tab:triage-stability} reports, per multi-benchmark requirement, the full score range reachable across all weightings.
The result is \textbf{non-degenerate}: of the three requirements with disagreeing contributing benchmarks, two change band under reweighting.
The mechanism is exact. Since $s_R$ in \Cref{eq:agg} is a convex combination of the contributing benchmark means, the achievable range under any weighting is bounded by their spread $\Delta=\max_b \overline{x}_b - \min_b \overline{x}_b$: $\mathrm{Range}\le\Delta$, with equality at the single-benchmark-dominant extreme.
$\Delta$ is therefore the \emph{sole} quantity that governs weight sensitivity, and our platform reports it directly (\Cref{tab:triage-stability}).
For CR01 the contributing benchmarks agree ($\Delta=0$, all $1.00$), so the score is \emph{provably} invariant to the weights---a negative the check certifies rather than assumes.
For capability CR06 and representation-bias CR10 the native benchmarks \emph{disagree} ($\Delta=1.00$: CR06 has \texttt{gsm8k}$=0$ against four benchmarks at $1.00$; CR10 has \texttt{bold}$=0$ against \texttt{bbq}/\texttt{stereoset} at $1.00$), so the reweighting range spans $[0,1]$ and crosses both band thresholds: the headline CR06 $=0.80$ (green) is one defensible weighting, but an auditor who weights mathematical reasoning (\texttt{gsm8k}) heavily would see it fall below amber.
RQ2's contribution is the \emph{reproducible mechanism}: weights are inspectable, $\Delta$ is reported per requirement, and the one-command re-aggregation surfaces precisely these band-flips with no new model queries.

\subsection{RQ3: Control-Room Interpretability}
We assess interpretability as a \emph{design validation}, not a user study: two authors performed a structured walkthrough of the dashboard (\Cref{fig:dashboard}) populated by the run, in guided (no-login) mode.
The status-first ordering places the weakest requirement (CR08, amber, widest interval) at the top, with passing requirements collapsed behind ``show all''; the coverage band, Trust Factor, and harness-provenance table are visible without scrolling; and ``fallback'' badges make the degraded harnesses unmissable.
The control room supported, in one or two clicks each, all eight triage tasks we posed---identify the weakest requirement, read its band and uncertainty, spot which benchmarks fell back, read the catalogue version and signature, judge coverage at a glance, reach a raw-output sample, distinguish failed vs.\ fallback vs.\ ok, and launch a new run without the CLI---versus minutes of correlating \texttt{benchmark\_run.yaml} and \texttt{raw\_outputs.jsonl} by hand.
Because the raters are the authors, we report this as evidence that the interface \emph{affords} these tasks, not as a measured satisfaction outcome; a formal panel with external raters and inter-rater reliability~\cite{hayes2007krippendorff,studtmann2023histree} is future work.

\subsection{Governance Runtime Performance}
\label{sec:gaas-eval}
To check that the governance-as-a-service runtime of \Cref{sec:gaas} is a working system and not only an architecture, we benchmarked its three load-bearing properties in-process against the real components (proxy, four agents, OPA layer, event bus), reusing the platform's own scoring code.
\emph{Overhead.} Measured as the governed-path time minus the forwarding time it wraps, the inline proxy adds a median $5.7$\,ms (p95 $8.2$\,ms) of governance machinery per request---about $2\%$ on top of a $280$\,ms local generation---so the synchronous gate is cheap relative to inference.
\emph{Detection.} On three planted abuse cases the agents fire correctly ($3/3$): the cyber agent flags a system-prompt-exfiltration jailbreak (signal $0.20$), the privacy agent flags a response leaking a synthetic IBAN, e-mail, and phone (Presidio, $0.20$), and the ethics agent flags an insulting reply (Detoxify, $0.01$)---each below the $0.70$ trust threshold.
\emph{Enforcement and degradation.} The OPA layer denies under \emph{enforcement} mode with the kill-switch engaged and allows a high-trust request under \emph{shadow}, and with no Kafka/Redpanda broker present the event bus transparently degraded to the Redis-Streams fallback and round-tripped a probe end-to-end---confirming the graceful-degradation paths of \Cref{fig:gaas} hold under test.

\section{Banking Deployment and Regulatory Mapping}
\label{sec:banking}
We ground RAIP in the setting that motivates it: a \emph{release gate} for a customer-facing retail-banking LLM assistant (account FAQs, product explanations, complaint triage). In the EU such a system is doubly regulated---by the AI Act, and, because it runs inside a financial institution, by sector rules a bank's second line of defence already enforces. RAIP's value is to answer, in one reproducible signed run, whether a model clears both the horizontal AI-Act bar and the sectoral bar.

\emph{Regulatory mapping.} The AI Act principles RAIP measures line up with concrete obligations under the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA)~\cite{dora2022}, the EBA ICT and security-risk guidelines~\cite{eba2021ictrisk}, and the French supervisor's (ACPR) work on AI in finance~\cite{acpr2020ai}. \Cref{tab:banking-map} sketches the correspondence: the same run that produces an AI-Act evidence pack also yields artifacts a banking auditor recognizes---ICT-risk and robustness evidence (DORA, EBA), privacy/data-governance evidence (GDPR, EBA), and the human-oversight and explainability attestations the ACPR expects.

\emph{Data-stage results on a banking corpus.} To exercise privacy and data governance concretely, RAIP scanned a \emph{fully synthetic} 230-document banking corpus (FR/EN retail, corporate, wealth, and complaint records; seeded script, no customer data) that plants the failure modes a bank cares about: synthetic IBANs (\texttt{FR76}\,+\,23 digits), RIBs, e-mails, and phones for privacy; near-duplicate pairs for copyright; and imbalanced client segments for data adequacy. The native engines scored privacy CR05 $=0.73$ (Presidio recall on the planted PII), copyright CR04 $=0.94$ (Levenshtein/BLEU near-duplicate detection), and data adequacy CR03 $=0.84$ (group-balance heuristics). These scores are model-independent---the scans run on the corpus, not the model---so they form a reusable data-governance baseline a bank re-runs whenever its retrieval corpus changes, justifying the gate in a banking context rather than only naming one.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\emph{Internal validity.}
The baseline catalogue weights (\texttt{mvp2-v1}) are author-designed; we mitigate this by making them an inspectable artifact and reporting sensitivity, but the set of comparison weightings (uniform, single-benchmark-dominant) is itself a design choice.
A judge=model configuration (Llama~3.1 8B) may bias the LLM-judged refusal/jailbreak probes.
The capability and representation-bias requirements (CR06/CR10) use RAIP's dynamic probes under the Ollama log-probability constraint rather than the native lm-eval log-likelihood harness; this is flagged in provenance, and a vLLM backend would let the native harness corroborate those two scores.
The interpretability walkthrough was performed by authors, risking confirmation bias.

\emph{External validity.}
Evidence centres on a panel of three open-weight models (3B--8B class) at $n=10$ items per benchmark; larger models, more items, or a log-probability backend may yield different scores and different sensitivity.
The banking corpus (\Cref{sec:banking}) is fully synthetic by design (no customer data), so CR03--CR05 demonstrate the data-stage engines rather than measure a production corpus.

\emph{Construct validity.}
COMPL-AI scores are technical proxies for regulatory obligations~\cite{guldimann2024complai,euaiact2024}, not legal conclusions.
The Trust Factor and the dashboard's bands are presentation aids for human arbitration, not automatic gates.
The audit-PDF signature is a verifiable digest, not a qualified eIDAS/RFC~3161 timestamp.

\emph{Tool limitations.}
The full lifecycle lab (dataset, checkpoint, poisoning) and the deeper benchmark/weighting science are out of this paper's scope.
The governance-runtime benchmark (\Cref{sec:gaas-eval}) exercises the real proxy, agents, policy layer, and bus in-process on planted cases; it measures per-request overhead and detection correctness, not throughput under sustained production load, which a field deployment would add.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Fragmented responsible-AI evaluation forces software teams to stitch benchmarks manually and hides the value judgements baked into score aggregation.
RAIP satisfies PR2--PR4 (traceability, reproducibility, sovereignty) in the evaluation reported here and \emph{implements} PR1 (lifecycle coverage), whose dataset and checkpoint stages we describe but do not exercise in this software-focused study.
It contributes an open-source, self-hostable platform that \emph{standardizes} responsible-AI evaluation into one control room (C1); end-to-end \emph{auditability and provenance}, with signed governance documents generated from each run and every number traceable to its scorer (C2); and---as the headline---the \emph{aggregation into a single score made inspectable and auditable}, the weights a versioned signed artifact with a reproducible sensitivity check (C3); validated by an empirical study across a three-model panel, grounded in a banking case study and its sector regulation.
We found that RAIP produces reproducible, interval-qualified, provenance-honest scores that discriminate across a three-model panel (RQ1); that weighting sensitivity is bounded by the within-requirement benchmark spread $\Delta$ and is here non-degenerate---CR01 provably invariant ($\Delta=0$), CR06 and CR10 flipping band ($\Delta=1.00$), so their verdict is weight-dependent and measured rather than asserted (RQ2); and that a compliance reader can triage a run from the control room alone (RQ3).
The replication package will provide the lite Compose manifest, the result and figure scripts, and the run artifacts.

Future work includes a formal practitioner study with inter-rater reliability~\cite{hayes2007krippendorff,studtmann2023histree}, native-harness runs that would stress the sensitivity analysis further, and a separate treatment of the deeper weighting and benchmark-selection science (empirical re-derivation of weights, contamination-aware refresh) that this software-focused paper deliberately defers.
