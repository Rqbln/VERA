\psection{threats}{0.4}{I1--I4}{core,dal}{Threats to Validity}
\textbf{Construct validity.}
Language-model benchmark scores are internal checks for comparing models within VERA, not leaderboard accuracies.
Fairness probes for language models are weak instruments~\cite{blodgett2021stereotyping,raji2021everything,reuel2024betterbench} that Section~\ref{sec:fairness} sets aside, yet the decision-study scorecard showed a fairness row from such a probe\decision{D11}{core,foundry}{18 Sep}{Which scorecard was sent on 14 Sep (pre-campaign values or the E6 freeze), and where are freeze.json and its timestamp?}; that study observes how reviewers use evidence, not whether the evidence is right.
Attack and toxicity verdicts inherit the biases of one open-weight judge served on Azure, which is also the model behind one target deployment and so grades its own outputs there.
A native cell means that no fallback was flagged, not that the instrument is valid.
Fairness criteria are technical proxies for non-discrimination law, not legal conclusions~\cite{wachter2021fairness}.

\textbf{Internal validity.}
The unaided phase of the decision study always came first, so a changed choice may partly reflect reconsideration.
The form never named the tool, to limit demand effects.
Respondents volunteered and some are colleagues of the authors, so the sample may favour structured evidence.
The form timestamps sections only, so decision time is not tested.
